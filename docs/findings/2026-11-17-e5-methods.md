# Finding: E5, on generated text only: a small model for personal data, taken at a low threshold, with the steps `titles` and `carry` on top, finds 1,593 of 1,595 planted names on selection and 424 of 425 on the held-out corpus; the model of the plan alone finds 92.92 and 91.06 percent. Seven of ten predictions failed, and three models were downloaded on a reading that the review does not accept as Achia's yes

This note is not about a gate that failed. It reports the first experiment of the series Achia decided on 2026-11-16, with `## Decision` left empty. It decides nothing: the model of Phase 3 stands until he decides otherwise.

Read the two limits first. They are as much the finding as the numbers.

1. **All text is generated, by one author, who wrote the steps after reading part of it.**
   - The steps that are no model were written after reading E4's errors, and E4's corpus is 1,145 of the 1,595 names on which methods were chosen. `select` and `heldout` were written before the steps were run on anything. The steps were then changed twice after they had been run on `select`: the trigger lists after a dry run with made-up detectors, and the trigger rule and `carry strong` after one table of `joint` on `select` was seen (`PREREGISTRATION.md`, first amendment). None of the five chosen methods uses `carry strong`. Nothing was changed after `heldout` was scored.
   - The steps that look for a title or a group word, and all three corpora, use the same closed list of titles. With no model at all, `titles` and `carry` together find 1,128 of the 1,595 names, 70.72 percent. So a number such as 99.87 percent is not the work of a model alone.
   - The held-out corpus is other text, but from the same author and the same list. It can catch a model, a threshold or `carry` that fits the selection text. It cannot judge `titles`.

## What was being measured

E4 measured the model of the plan (`dicta-il/dictabert-joint`) at 1,084 of 1,145 planted names, with 51 of its 61 misses in two kinds of mention: a surname after `משפחת` or `האחים`, and a given name alone that is an everyday word. Achia asked for a series of experiments "to get the best most fit tools" before the tests demand anything for those kinds (`build-plan.md` section 11), and on 2026-11-17 for a short list of candidates per part and experiments "to see who are the winners" (quoted in `experiments/e5_methods/PREREGISTRATION.md`).

E5 asks which way of finding names misses fewest: another model, several models together, or steps that are no model. It measures, for each method:

- **recall** by E4's strict rule, on names a script planted and wrote down;
- **the words it marks for nothing**, which the product would replace too;
- **seconds and peak memory** on the laptop of `docs/REFERENCE_MACHINE.md`.

The text: three generated corpora. A method is **chosen** on two (`e4`, E4's 40 documents, and `select`, 20 new documents: 1,595 names). The chosen methods are **judged once** on a third (`heldout`, 20 documents from other templates and other names: 425 names). Everything is in `experiments/e5_methods/`; the plan of the experiment, with two dated amendments, is `PREREGISTRATION.md`.

**What was run.** Four detectors: `joint` (the baseline), `golem` (`CordwainerSmith/GolemPII-v2-small`), `large` (`dicta-il/dictabert-large-ner`), `seter_fast` (`shoresh-labs/seter-pii-he-ar-fast`). Five steps that are no model: `group` (the word after `משפחת`, `האחים`), `titles` (also the word after a personal title), `carry` (a name found once is marked wherever else it stands in the document, also behind attached letters), `carry strong` (the same, only for a name found twice or as two words), `list` (every word on a public list of first names). Lower thresholds for the three models that give a chance per token; the plan's model gives none here, so it has no threshold row. Every union of two detectors, and of all four. 70 methods without a list, 8 with each of two lists.

**What was not run**, and why: `seter` (the larger model) and the 8-bit form of `seter_fast`, because downloading stopped after the review's finding; GLiNER multi v2.1, Stanza and a small language model, because each needs a Python package the experiments do not have, and adding a package is Achia's decision; fine-tuning; the prefix forms of E3; real documents.

### What is still missed, and what is marked for nothing

- **The winner's misses**, three in 2,020 mentions over all three corpora: each is an initial with a surname after a full stop or a colon (`ר' שפירא`, `ר' נגר`, `ד' להב`), marked in part and so not counted as found. No whole surname and no given name is missed.
- **The baseline with `titles, carry`** misses five on selection: the same two initials, two surnames that stand after `עד לחתימה:` (`שדה`, `הכהן`), and half of a full name whose surname is an everyday word (`כנרת שדה`).
- **Extra words of the winner on selection**, 55 without titles. From the model at its low threshold, 35: `לבין` 15 times ("and between", in `בין X לבין Y`), `מהאדרה` 5, `ולבנה` 5, `שלום` 5, `הואיל` 3, and two more. From `titles`, 5: `האחראית`, the word after `האחות`. From `carry`, 15: `תום`, `בתום`, `אשר`, `למרות`, `ולמרות`, `והואיל`, `מרשי`. That is the price of `carry`: once a word is marked as a name somewhere in a document, the same word is marked wherever it stands, also where it is the everyday word. Seven of those 15 spread a word the model had marked wrongly: `והואיל` 3 times, which `golem` marks at its own choice too; `מרשי` and `למרשי` 4 times, which only the low threshold marks. `joint + carry` pays 8 words (`תום`, `בתום`, `אשר`, `למרות`, `ולמרות`).
- **A lower threshold** was measured on the three models that give a chance. On `golem`, 0.03 finds 60 more names than the model's own choice for 6 more extra words. On `seter_fast` it finds 27 more for 21, on `large` 54 more for 53. It was not measured on the plan's model.

## What this rules in and what it rules out

All of it on this text.

**Rules in.**

- On this text, a second pass that carries a found name to its other places adds more to the plan's model than a second model does: 92.92 to 99.50 percent on selection for 8 extra words, against 98.75 for the best pair of models. That is a result on selection, on text where every family surname stands again elsewhere in its document.
- `golem` is a better detector than the plan's model on this text: 95.24 against 92.92 percent on selection, 95.06 against 91.06 held out. It took 173 seconds against 602 on selection and peaked at 856 MB against 1,191 MB, one run each. Its licence is MIT.
- On `golem`, a threshold of 0.03 is cheap: 1,579 names, 99.00%, for 6 more extra words.

**Rules out, on this text.**

- A list of first names as a detector, in the form tried here.
- `large` as a replacement for the plan's model, as far as its `PER` label goes.
- More models as the way to the last points: all four together find one name more than one model with `carry`.

**What it cannot say.**

- **Anything about real documents.** The corpora are 80 invented documents by one author. A real contract names a person once in a signature block, uses titles that are not on the list, and breaks a name across a line. The plan's held-out batch of real documents (section 7) is where this must be measured.
- **How much `carry` does alone on other text.** Held out it was scored only with `titles`. In that stack the names with no title or group word before them go from 251 to 262 of 265 on `joint`, and from 249 to 262 on `golem`; only `carry` reaches such a name, but it may carry one that `titles` gave.
- **Whether the rule for group words is still needed.** In this text a family's surname always stands again elsewhere in its document.
- **How much of `titles` survives outside the list.** Every title in every corpus is on the step's own list. For `joint` and `golem`, `carry` and the threshold do not depend on that list; of the five methods judged on `heldout`, the three with `titles` do.
- **A person named once.** The corpora hold 120 such mentions and the winner finds all of them. By the reviewer's count most stand in a full sentence; 15 of the 65 on selection do not (signature lines, lines after `עד לחתימה:`, address lines). Achia's decision of 2026-11-16 to give "a name that stands once" a line of its own is not answered by these.
- **What `carry` costs on real text.** It marks an everyday word wherever it stands once a person of that name is in the document. Here that is a handful of words. In a long judgment about a person named `שלום` it could be many.
- **Speed on a quiet machine**, and anything about `seter`, the 8-bit model, GLiNER, Stanza or a language model.

## Options, with the cost of each

1. **Keep the plan as it is: `dictabert-joint` in Phase 3, no step.** No cost now. On this text it leaves 7 to 9 names in 100 unmarked, most of them in the two kinds E4 named.
2. **Put `carry` into the plan as a step to be built and measured**, after detection, on whatever model Phase 3 uses. It is a few dozen lines with no model and no dependency. Phase 6 already speaks of an entity's other mentions. It needs its own tests on real documents, because both its gain and its cost, everyday words marked, show only there, and E5 did not check it on text it was not chosen on.
3. **Also change the model of Phase 3 to `golem`, or run both.** `golem` alone is the better detector here. Changing the model is a decision of its own: a new model in the product, its licence (MIT) and its card read by Achia, and E1's ten real judgments run again with it, since E1 is the only real text there is. The download question comes first.
4. **Run the rest of the short list before deciding 3:** `seter`, the 8-bit model, GLiNER, Stanza, the small language model. Each needs his yes for a download, three need a package. How long it takes was not estimated from anything measured.
5. **Measure on real documents before any of 2 to 4.** The held-out batch of the plan does not exist yet; choosing its documents is his (issue #4).

## Recommendation

Option 2 as a plan item, option 5 as the next measurement, option 3 only after it.

What the numbers carry: on generated text, `carry` on the plan's model was the largest single gain on selection, and the full stack with `titles` and `carry` held on other generated text. What they do not carry: that `carry` alone holds on new text, that it holds on real text, or what it costs there. So the recommendation is to plan it and measure it, not to adopt it.

The model question is smaller than it looks on this text: with `titles` and `carry` on top, the plan's model and `golem` find the same number of names, 1,590 of 1,595 on selection and 422 of 425 held out, though not the same names. What separates them here is time, 173 seconds against 602 on selection, and memory, 856 MB against 1,191 MB. That is worth a decision only after it has been seen on real text.

A lower threshold is what takes `golem` with `titles` and `carry` from 422 to 424 held out. It was not measured on the plan's model.
